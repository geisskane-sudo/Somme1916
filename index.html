How the simulation works

The front is cut into 100 m stretches. Each has the German front, support and reserve trenches, intermediate lines and the second position. Each British company advances along its stretch at walking pace, or rushes if it crept out early. German riflemen and machine guns fire on men in the open, and strongpoints fire into neighbouring stretches. German guns shell no man's land and the British trenches. When the barrage lifts, the Germans race up from their dugouts to the parapet, and the attackers must get there first. Companies that lose men too fast go to ground in shell holes, and crawl back after dark. Captured trenches are bombed from the flanks and counter-attacked by German reserves. They hold only while bombs can be carried across no man's land. Each division's casualties, and the ground the record says it held at key times, are fixed from the record; the simulation decides how the day gets there. The 49th Division and other units not shown account for about 640 of the 57,470 casualties.
